Most people who get hacked aren't targeted by clever attackers. They fall to a reused password, a convincing message, or an unpatched device. Five habits cover most of that risk.

## 1. Use a password manager

A password manager creates and remembers a long, unique password for every account. Reusing one password everywhere means one leak unlocks everything. You then only need to remember one strong master passphrase.

## 2. Turn on multi-factor authentication

Start with email, banking and social media. An app-based code or a hardware key is stronger than an SMS code, but any second factor is much better than none. Your email account matters most, because it can reset every other password.

## 3. Pause before clicking

Phishing messages create urgency: "Your account will be closed", "Payment failed", "Package waiting". Before you click:

- Check the sender's real address, not just the display name.
- Hover over links to see where they really go.
- If a message worries you, open the website yourself or call the number on your card, not the one in the message.

## 4. Keep devices updated

Updates fix the security holes attackers actually use. Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer, browser and router.

## 5. Back up what matters

Ransomware and lost devices are far less painful if you have a recent backup. Keep one copy that is not always connected to your computer, such as an external drive or a cloud backup with version history.

## Start small

Do habit 2 for your email today. Add one more habit each week. Security is a routine, not a one-time project.
