In June 2024 I attended Cisco Live in Las Vegas. A week of sessions, labs and conversations is easy to enjoy and just as easy to forget, so I wrote down what I learned and shared a summary with my management afterwards. Here is the useful part, along with what I'd tell anyone planning to attend a conference.

## What I attended

The sessions I completed were:

- **Is Your Organization AI-Ready?** How to deploy and manage AI-powered technologies rapidly and safely.
- **AI Enablement Framework:** how Cisco itself approaches AI internally.
- **Cognitive SNOC, powered by Cisco XDR and Splunk:** security operations with AI-assisted detection and response.
- **Demystifying Wi-Fi Throughput in Meraki Environments:** a troubleshooting-focused session.
- **Troubleshoot Contact Center AI:** support-focused technical content.
- **Lessons Learned in Custom Zero-Trust Training Development:** building practical zero-trust skills.
- **Cisco Networking Innovation Summit:** the opening session and the data center session.
- **A tour of the Cisco Store Tech Lab**, plus time at the **Technical Assistance Center**.

## Five takeaways

1. **AI is moving from slides to products.** The most valuable sessions were the ones about deploying AI *rapidly and safely*, meaning governance and security alongside the technology.
2. **Hands-on beats slides.** Labs and troubleshooting sessions turn a feature name into something you can actually use on Monday morning.
3. **Strategy sessions are worth the time.** Keynotes and summits help you decide where your own roadmap should and shouldn't follow the trend.
4. **Security is the thread that connects everything.** Almost every topic, from Wi-Fi to AI to data center, came back to visibility, identity and response.
5. **People are the real return.** Conversations with engineers from other industries gave me practical ideas that no session covered.

## How to get more from any conference

- **Plan your agenda before you arrive.** Mix strategy sessions, technical deep dives and hands-on labs, rather than only the topics you already know.
- **Take notes in one place.** Write two or three actions per session, not a transcript.
- **Visit the support and engineering teams.** Talking with the people who fix problems all day is often more valuable than a keynote.
- **Write a one-page summary within a week.** Cover what you learned, what you'd change, and what you recommend. It turns attendance into value for your team, and it helps you remember.
- **Follow up with the people you met** while the conversation is still fresh.
